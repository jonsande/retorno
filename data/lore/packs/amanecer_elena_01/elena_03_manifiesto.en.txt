THE MANIFEST

UNSENT PRIVATE MESSAGE — E. VARELA
Date: 17 years after Operation AMANECER

Tomás:

Salcedo came by today. He asked me about the sixth transport. He says they're reviewing the AMANECER records. I don't know who "they" are. He wouldn't tell me.

I reminded him that the commission closed the investigation fifteen years ago. Then he took out a copy of the manifest and asked me to count the passengers. There were twelve names. I told him I already knew that list.

He asked why my signature appeared on the departure authorization. I told him it was my job. Then he pointed out something I'd never noticed. Beneath my signature there is a second authorization, entered forty-three seconds after mine.

I don't know whose it is. Salcedo says he doesn't either. I don't believe him.

I almost told him about the transmission. The one we received six hours later, when there was no one left at the station. But I kept quiet.

I don't want to talk about any of it again. I don't want to face nineteen people waiting for me to tell them who lives and who stays behind.
